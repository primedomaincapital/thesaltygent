From Deck to Dinner: The Boat Shoe's Quiet Comeback

DECK TO DINNER · 2026  ·  4 min read

The boat shoe was invented by accident, and it has spent ninety years trying to live down the accident. In 1935, the sailor Paul Sperry slipped on the deck of his boat and noticed his dog, Prince, whose paws were naturally non-slip, did not. Sperry cut grooves into a rubber sole modeled on the dog's paw, and the Top-Sider was born. A few years later, the deck shoe was adopted as the official footwear of the U.S. Navy, and when President John F. Kennedy wore them casually in the 1960s, they cemented their place as the preppy wardrobe's anchor — a symbol of coastal style, casual sophistication, and summer ease.

The shoe spent the better part of a decade parked in the preppy bin alongside popped collars and pastel chinos, a shorthand for a look that had gone stale. Then it came back. Boat shoes walked the Spring 2026 runways, and the best pairs are now selling as the antidote to the chunky sneaker: more polished than canvas kicks, more comfortable than leather loafers, and versatile enough to carry a man from the deck to the boardwalk to the bar. Search interest for boat shoes spikes every June and hit a five-year high in summer 2024, with the trend continuing strong into 2025, and Pinterest reported a 35 percent year-over-year increase in pins for boat shoes outfits.

The brands that matter

Every boat shoe owes something to the original Sperry design from 1935. The Authentic Original, in full-grain leather with 360-degree lacing and moccasin construction, is still the purest version of the idea, and Sperry holds roughly 45 percent of the boat shoe market, according to a 2025 Footwear News survey, followed by Timberland at 22 percent and Sebago at 15 percent. Timberland's Authentic 3-Eye Lug swaps the traditional razor-thin sole for a chunky lug outsole, better suited to wandering from the marina to the pub along the coastal walk than to a wet deck.

The quieter end of the market is where the connoisseurs shop. Quoddy's Classic Boat Shoe, unlined in whiskey cavalier leather, was worn by the Kennedys, Steve McQueen, and Paul Newman, and it remains the reference point for a handmade American deck shoe. R.M. Williams, which has spent close to a century perfecting footwear for the Australian outback, makes the Dockyard moccasin boat shoe. OluKai's Moku Pae brings modern comfort to the silhouette, and Frescobol Carioca's Marina deck shoe, set on a Vibram rubber sole, sits in the sweet spot between heritage American pairs and luxury heavyweights. Suede boat shoes have seen a 40 percent increase in search interest since 2023, driven by the quiet luxury trend.

How to wear them without the costume

The rules are simple, and breaking them is how a good shoe becomes a joke. Pair them with tailored shorts that sit just above the knee in neutral colors, or with classic prints of madras or seersucker, for weekend activities, summer dinner parties, and beach getaways. For a more formal look, pair them with an Oxford shirt and chinos — brown, classic navy, and burgundy leather boat shoes pair well with olive, navy, khaki, and stone-colored chinos. The boat shoe sits in a Venn diagram between dressy loafers and casual canvas kicks, and that is its strength: it is the shoe for a schedule that runs from beach days to the boardwalk to the bar.

The sock question has a definitive answer in 2026, and it is the no-show sock. The intended look is sockless, creating a clean summery line from leg to shoe, and the no-show sock is non-negotiable. Under no circumstances should boat shoes be worn with white athletic crew socks. That is the cardinal sin, and it is the fastest way to turn a heritage shoe into a punchline.

The counterargument: the costume risk is real

The case against the boat shoe is the same as the case against every preppy revival: it can read as a costume, a man dressed as an idea of a sailor rather than a man who sails. The popped collar, the pastel chinos, the boat shoe worn with a suit — each is a small failure of judgment, and together they are a uniform for a life the wearer does not lead. GQ's 2025 style guide is explicit: boat shoes are functional footwear for wet environments, while loafers are designed for dry, formal settings, and the two should not be confused. Boat shoes with a suit are never correct. The formal structure of a suit requires a dress shoe, an oxford or a derby, and no amount of coastal confidence changes that.

The answer is to wear the shoe where it belongs. On the deck, on the dock, on the boardwalk, at the harbor restaurant where the floor is wood and the dress code is unspoken. A boat shoe worn by a man who has actually been on a boat looks like what it is: a tool, worn well. A boat shoe worn as a statement looks like a statement, and statements age badly.

Ninety years after a sailor watched his dog run across the ice, the shoe is still doing the job it was designed for. The comeback is not a trend. It is a reminder that the best coastal dressing was never about fashion at all. It was about footwear that works, worn by men who know where they are going — from the deck to dinner, in the same pair.
